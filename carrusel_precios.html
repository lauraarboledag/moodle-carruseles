02 / 12  ·  🔢 NUMBERS 🔢

Numbers 1-100

How much does it cost? Numbers and currencies

To talk about prices you need to know numbers. Here are some basic ones and how they are used with currencies.

1-10: uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez

11-20: once, doce, trece, catorce, quince, dieciséis...

20-100: veinte, treinta, cuarenta, cincuenta, sesenta, setenta, ochenta, noventa, cien

Common currencies: euro (€), dollar ($), Mexican peso (MX$), Colombian peso (COL$)

Examples: 5 euros, 10 dollars, 20 pesos

💬 EXAMPLES

"El café cuesta dos euros."
"Son treinta y cinco pesos."

03 / 12  ·  ❓ QUESTIONS ❓

Phrases to ask

¿Cuánto cuesta? / ¿Cuánto vale?

Learn the most common ways to ask for the price in Spanish.

¿Cuánto cuesta? (singular)

How much does it cost?

¿Cuánto cuestan? (plural)

How much do they cost?

¿Cuánto vale? (singular)

How much is it worth?

¿Cuánto valen? (plural)

How much are they?

¿Cuánto es? / ¿Cuánto es en total?

How much is it? / How much is the total?

💬 EXAMPLES

"¿Cuánto cuesta este vestido?"
"¿Cuánto valen estos zapatos?"

09 / 12  ·  🌎 CULTURE 🌎

Haggling and tips

Shopping customs

In many Hispanic countries, haggling is common in markets and informal shops. Tipping also varies by country.

Regatear

to haggle (common in Mexico, Colombia, etc.)

¿Me hace un descuento?

Can you give me a discount?

¿Es lo último?

Is that your best price?

Propina (10% en restaurantes, opcional)

tip (10% in restaurants, optional)

💡 FACT

In Spain, tipping is not mandatory; it's usually a few coins. In Mexico and other Latin American countries, it's sometimes included in the bill.

11 / 12  ·  🚨 ERRORS 🚨

Watch out!

Common mistakes with prices and quantities

❌ "¿Cuánto cuestan este libro?"

✅ "¿Cuánto cuesta este libro?" (singular)

❌ "Son dos euros y medio."

✅ "Son dos con cincuenta" or "dos euros con cincuenta céntimos".

❌ "Quiero un kilo manzanas."

✅ "Quiero un kilo de manzanas." (preposition "de")

❌ "El cambio es 5 dólar."

✅ "El cambio es 5 dólares." (plural)
