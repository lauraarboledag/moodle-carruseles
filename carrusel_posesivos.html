What is a possessive?

Possessive adjectives indicate who owns something. In this block we will study the short forms: mi, tu and su, which go before the noun.

Tu

02 / 11 👤

Possessive 1 of 3

MI / MIS

my — belongs to the speaker (yo)

MI is used with singular nouns. MIS is used with plural nouns. The possessive agrees with the thing possessed, not with the owner.

SINGULAR

mi

one object / one person

PLURAL

mis

several objects / several people

📋 EXAMPLES WITH MI / MIS

mi casa · mi perro · mi mamá

singular

mis libros · mis amigos · mis llaves

plural

"Mi apartamento está en el centro."

"Mis padres viven en Medellín."

💡 IMPORTANT: It does NOT change by gender

mi libro (masc.) = mi casa (fem.) → always the same

03 / 11 ✍️

Key spelling

MI without accent vs. MÍ with accent

A very common mistake you should avoid

possessive adjective

mi

Goes before a noun.
my

"Mi nombre es Ana."

"Mi familia es grande."

personal pronoun

mí

Pronoun after a preposition.
me / myself

"Para mí, el español es fácil."

"Ese libro es para mí."

🧪 TRICK TO REMEMBER IT

If you can replace it with "nuestro/a", it's the possessive mi (without accent).
If it goes after a preposition (para, de, por, sin, con…), it's the pronoun mí (with accent).

⚠️ "a mí me gusta" → pronoun ✅ · "mi libro" → possessive ✅

04 / 11 👥

Possessive 2 of 3

TU / TUS

your (informal) — belongs to the person you're talking to (tú)

TU is used with singular nouns. TUS is used with plural nouns. It is the possessive of trust: used with people you address informally.

SINGULAR

tu

one object / one person

PLURAL

tus

several objects / several people

📋 EXAMPLES WITH TU / TUS

tu carro · tu hermano · tu dirección

singular

tus hijos · tus fotos · tus cosas

plural

"¿Cómo se llama tu ciudad natal?"

"Tus ideas son muy creativas."

💡 SAME AS MI: does not change by gender

tu libro (masc.) = tu mochila (fem.) → always the same

05 / 11 ✍️

Key spelling

TU without accent vs. TÚ with accent

The most common confusion in written Spanish

possessive adjective

tu

Goes before a noun.
your

"Tu número de teléfono."

"Tu opinión me importa."

personal pronoun

tú

Subject of the sentence.
you

"Tú hablas muy bien."

"¿Tú eres estudiante?"

🧪 TRICK TO REMEMBER IT

If you can replace it with "mi/mis", it's the possessive tu (without accent).
If it is the subject of the sentence, you can replace it with "yo" → it's the pronoun tú (with accent).

⚠️ "Tú tienes razón — tu idea es buena." → both in the same sentence

06 / 11 👤👤

Possessive 3 of 3

SU / SUS

his / her / its / your (formal) / their

SU/SUS is the most versatile possessive — it corresponds to multiple subjects: él, ella, usted, ellos, ellas, ustedes. SU = singular · SUS = plural.

👥 WHO DOES SU/SUS BELONG TO?

él

his

ella

her

usted

your (formal)

ellos

their (masc.)

ellas

their (fem.)

ustedes

your (plural)

📋 EXAMPLES WITH SU / SUS

"Carlos vive con su familia." (his)

"María y sus amigas van al cine." (her)

"¿Cuál es su nombre, señor?" (your, formal)

07 / 11 ❓

Important point

SU: when there is ambiguity

How to clarify who something belongs to

Since SU can refer to several people, sometimes the context isn't enough. In those cases we use article + noun + de + pronoun to clarify.

🔍 HOW TO CLARIFY AMBIGUITY

❓ Ambiguous:

"Su casa es grande." → whose?

✅ Clear alternatives:

"La casa de él es grande."

"La casa de ella es grande."

"La casa de usted es grande."

"La casa de ellos es grande."

✅ CONTEXT SOLVES EVERYTHING:

"Carlos llegó tarde. Su jefe estaba enojado."
→ It's clear that it's Carlos's boss.

💡 In spoken conversation, context almost always clarifies who the owner is. The long form is only needed when writing or in formal contexts.

08 / 11 ⚖️

Golden rule

Agreement: with the thing, not with the owner

The biggest difference vs. English

In Spanish, the possessive agrees with the noun it accompanies (singular/plural), not with the gender of the owner. This is different from English.

📊 SPANISH VS. ENGLISH

SPANISH

ENGLISH

Carlos: "su mamá"

"his mom"

Ana: "su mamá"

"her mom"

Carlos: "sus libros"

"his books"

Ana: "sus libros"

"her books"

In Spanish Carlos and Ana use exactly the same possessive: su / sus

💡 The possessive changes only for singular ↔ plural of the thing possessed, never by the owner's gender.

10 / 11 🚨

Mistakes and practice

Common mistakes + model dialogue

🚨 COMMON MISTAKES

❌ MISTAKE 1 — Wrong accent mark

"¿Cómo se llama tú hermano?"

✅ "¿Cómo se llama tu hermano?"

❌ MISTAKE 2 — Wrong plural

"Mi amigos son de Colombia."

✅ "Mis amigos son de Colombia."

❌ MISTAKE 3 — Owner's gender

"Ana llegó con sua hija."

✅ "Ana llegó con su hija." ("sua" doesn't exist)

💬 MODEL DIALOGUE

"¿Cómo se llama tu ciudad?"
"Mi ciudad se llama Bogotá."
"¿Y tus padres viven allí también?"
"Sí, mis padres y mis hermanos."
"¿Cuál es su profesión?" (formal)
"Mi profesión es la enseñanza."
