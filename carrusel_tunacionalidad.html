Introduction

What does nationality mean?

Nationality indicates the country to which you legally belong (your legal link with a State). It's common information in introductions, forms, and conversations about identity.

Note:

It's important to distinguish country (place) from nationality (in many cases the ending -o/-a: mexicano / mexicana).

 
02 / 07 ❓

Asking

Common ways to ask

👋

"¿De dónde eres?"

Informal — very common among friends

🎩

"¿De dónde es usted?"

Formal — when speaking with strangers or in professional settings

Another direct way

"¿Cuál es tu nacionalidad?" / "¿Cuál es su nacionalidad?"

 
03 / 07 💬

Responding

Natural ways to respond

"Soy" + nationality. — Example: "Soy peruano / Soy peruana."

"Soy de" + country/city. — Example: "Soy de Lima."

"Vengo de" + country/city. — Example: "Vengo de México."

Tip: when talking about people, use the correct gender and number forms: Él es argentino, ellas son españolas.

 
04 / 07 🗣️

Dialogues

Practical examples (informal & formal)

INFORMAL

Sofía: "¿De dónde eres?"
Mateo: "Soy de Chile, ¿y tú?"
Sofía: "Soy peruana."

FORMAL

Funcionario: "¿Cuál es su nacionalidad?"
Visitante: "Soy española."

06 / 07 💭

Useful expressions

Phrases to talk about nationality

"Tengo doble nacionalidad" — To indicate two nationalities.

"Nací en..." — To talk about place of birth (not always the same as nationality).

"Mi nacionalidad es..." — More formal/direct way.

"Soy de padres..." — To talk about your family's origin: "Soy de padres colombianos"
